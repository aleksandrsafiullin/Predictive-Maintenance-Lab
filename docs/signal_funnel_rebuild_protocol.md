## Current learned implementation

GRU and LSTM optimize causal recurrent encoders and probabilistic path/event heads. Quantile Boosting fits Train-only, per-horizon physical-group-balanced quantile and censored-event classifiers, then optimizes a neural joint readout. Full MaleCNS uses every source neuron and retained connection in its causal graph encoder, followed by a learned probabilistic readout. The Full graph weights and fitted trees are fixed during readout training; claims of end-to-end neural optimization apply to recurrent models only.

The joint sampler first draws a first recorded-grid RED bucket or survival beyond the saved horizon. Its positive path transforms enforce the same first-entry bucket. Post-entry paths can decrease and recross freely. Already-RED origins have unrestricted positive future paths and no applicable first-entry target. This is an explicit, trainable event/path coupling, rather than a corridor inferred from independent marginal signal bounds.

The objective scores joint energy, simultaneous-band width, worst observed excursion outside that band, compatible first-entry/survival likelihood, and an auxiliary phase energy score. Width and miss penalties use frozen RED-normalized physical signal units. Discrete bucket draws receive unbiased within-row score-function gradients; independent within-row baselines avoid contamination from other origins' large post-RED errors. Targets are masked before arithmetic; missing suffixes, gaps and censoring never become fabricated zeros or negative event labels.

`path_objective_horizons_s` optionally freezes a list of supported saved-grid prefix horizons. One full joint path draw feeds equally weighted prefix energy/width/miss objectives and their categorical gradients. `event_objective_horizons_s` separately freezes equal-weight first-entry likelihood prefixes: later entries and full-grid survival collapse to survival through the shorter prefix, while earlier censoring retains compatible early buckets and survival. Both default to `None`, preserving existing full-horizon scoring. Conditional phase energy remains on the full grid. Train fitting and Validation checkpoint selection share the exact frozen objective. These independent settings permit controlled path and event horizon-alignment experiments while retaining the long output grid.

Keep all recordings from the same physical equipment group in one partition, including fragments and repeated cycles. Distribute gradual degradation, abrupt jumps, stable cases, operating regimes, recording lengths, gaps and observed events as representatively as the independent group counts allow. Inference receives only measurements and admitted metadata at or before the issue time. Learn residual dependence from grouped out-of-fold Train predictions, not fitted-training errors. Reset state at unit boundaries and gaps.

Hyperparameters, epoch selection and stopping use grouped cross-validation inside Train. Validation then serves calibration. An alternative is two disjoint, physical-group Validation subsets for selection and calibration, if there are enough groups. Freeze roles, thresholds, horizons and estimands before fitting. Test supplies no tuning or calibration feedback.

## Forecast and crossing contract

Save a versioned artifact with horizon grid, joint sampler, seed policy, preprocessing, physical-group split identity, threshold rule identity, calibration artifact and hashes. Return sampled paths `[sample, horizon]`, band bounds, issue time and current signal; preserve existing point outputs for compatibility.

Every path starts at the actual current observation. At time zero the displayed band collapses to that observation; this conditions on the measured signal, not an inferred noise-free state. Future bounds follow estimated uncertainty and may narrow or widen. Do not impose a geometric cone, monotone degradation, inevitable RED entry or a visually convenient width.

Errors must be temporally correlated. Independent draws from horizon quantiles, or crossings of lower/upper envelopes, do not identify a first-passage distribution. A bounded first implementation can sample whole residual vectors from grouped out-of-fold Train forecasts, with a documented conditional scale/dependence model. It remains an approximation requiring validation; scarce complete residual paths or regime support must return insufficient support. Missing suffixes must never become zero residuals. Any recursive extension samples and feeds back each complete synthetic path, propagating its uncertainty; no actual future observations are admitted.

For each sampled path, apply the causal RED rule frozen at issue time and find the first crossing bracket `(last observed-grid non-RED time, first grid RED time]`. Do not claim exact continuous crossing or assume linear interpolation as fact. Sparse sampling can miss a crossing and return; label the target as first recorded-grid RED entry unless an independently supported continuous-time observation model is added. An observation gap makes the physical first entry unresolved; never bridge it into a precise target.

Retain probability mass for no entry through the supported horizon. Output `probability_within_horizon`, `no_entry_probability`, conditional crossing-time bounds and their conditioning explicitly. A conditional interval among crossing paths is not an unconditional warning with the same coverage. If mass at no entry prevents a finite nominal interval, report unavailable or an open bound, never renormalize silently. Distinguish already RED, no entry within horizon, insufficient support, censoring and gaps.

## Learning, calibration and evaluation

Use proper losses that reward sharp, reliable predictions. For observed scalar targets, pinball loss learns quantiles; the interval score at miscoverage `alpha` is

`(upper - lower) + 2/alpha * (lower - actual)_+ + 2/alpha * (actual - upper)_+`.

Width is therefore penalized even when a target falls inside. For complete trajectory vectors, use a proper joint score such as the energy score `E||X-y|| - 0.5 E||X-X'||`, with fixed Train-derived scales, alongside marginal pinball/interval diagnostics. Estimate it with joint path samples. Predeclare treatment of incomplete vectors; scoring only complete paths changes the evaluated population and can bias results. Do not fabricate missing targets. These scoring principles are supported by [Gneiting and Raftery, sections 5 and 6.2](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf).

Observed RED evidence is a bracket, not its midpoint. If an auxiliary event-distribution head is trained, its loss uses probability mass in the admitted bracket, and survival probability for valid right censoring. Observation gaps and invalid follow-up remain unknown. Implement this only with a specified observation model and likelihood; an arbitrary overlap reward is not a proper score for censored events. Keep the event distribution consistent with the trajectory sampler, or report it separately.

Calibrate pointwise bounds separately from simultaneous full-path coverage. For simultaneous bands, predefine a maximum-over-horizons nonconformity score and a physical-group calibration design, including how replay origins are selected or aggregated. Thousands of overlapping windows are not thousands of independent calibration units. Marginal conformal quantile guarantees require exchangeability and do not automatically guarantee an entire path or event corridor; see [Romano et al., sections 1–4](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8613-conformalized-quantile-regression.pdf). Regime shifts and small group counts must remain explicit limitations.

Report point coverage by horizon, whole-path coverage on fully observed supported paths, band width, proper joint score, RED bracket containment/overlap separately, crossing probability calibration, no-entry mass, useful lead and false alerts. Publish independent group/event counts and unknown/censored denominators. If finite-sample calibration cannot support a requested level or useful width, mark insufficient calibration; do not force a narrow “calibrated 90%” band. Nominal coverage, useful lead and maximum acceptable width are user operational preferences to configure; no numeric preference has been approved here.
